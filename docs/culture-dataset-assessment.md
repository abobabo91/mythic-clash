# Mythic Clash Culture Dataset Assessment

Status: current figure-pool fit against the culture-family model

Assessment date: 2026-05-03

This document answers one question:

- looking at the current characters in the existing culture files, do they already support the readable competitive faction model well?

Short answer:

- partly yes
- but not cleanly enough yet

The current dataset contains enough mythic material to support the model, but many cultures are still too broad, too repetitive, or too weakly tagged to communicate a clear gameplay identity at a glance.

## Bottom-Line Judgment

The current characters are:

- good enough as source material
- not yet clean enough as competitive gameplay distribution

That means the project does **not** need to rebuild everything from scratch.

But it **does** need curation.

## What Looks Good Already

### 1. There are enough standout characters for the signature-card plan

The current legendary spread is workable.

Observed distribution:

- 79 cultures have 2 legendary figures
- 12 cultures have 3 legendary figures
- 5 cultures have 4 legendary figures
- a small number have 1, 5, or 6

This is enough to choose:

- one clear `Signature Character` per culture

without inventing that slot from nothing.

### 2. Many cultures already hint at readable gameplay families

Some cultures already feel naturally aligned to a primary family.

Examples:

- `Yazidi` reads strongly `Sanctuary`
- `Taiwanese Aboriginal` reads strongly `Wildhost`
- `Inuit` reads strongly `Tidebound`
- `Georgian Nart` reads strongly `Vanguard`
- `Zoroastrian Persian` reads strongly `Sanctuary`
- `Haida` reads strongly `Tidebound`
- `Zapotec` reads strongly `Wildhost`

That is encouraging, because it means the family model is not artificial. It already exists in the material.

### 3. The culture pool has enough thematic breadth

The big family buckets are all represented somewhere in the data:

- war/combat cultures
- storm/spell cultures
- healing/protection cultures
- death/underworld cultures
- sea/moon/water cultures
- wisdom/prophecy cultures
- fertility/beast/wild cultures
- trickster/illusion cultures

So the problem is not lack of range.

The problem is clarity.

## What Is Weak Right Now

### 1. Too many cultures are spread across too many families

In a quick pass, only about a third of cultures read as having a clearly dominant primary family, and even fewer have a clean secondary leaning.

That means many cultures currently feel like:

- flavor-rich collections

more than:

- readable gameplay identities

Examples of broad cultures:

- `Ancient Greek` currently reads as `Vanguard + Tempest + Oracle`
- `Ancient Egyptian` reads as `Vanguard + Sanctuary + Gravebound`
- `Aztec / Mexica` reads as `Wildhost + Tempest + Gravebound`
- `Apache` reads as `Sanctuary + Vanguard + Tempest`
- `Celtic Welsh` reads as `Vanguard + Oracle`

These are good themes, but for gameplay they still need one stronger center.

### 2. A lot of ability text is heavily repeated

This is one of the biggest practical problems.

There are many repeated ability descriptions across the full dataset, and some cultures have a lot of internal duplication too.

That causes two issues:

- cultures feel less mechanically distinct
- many cards communicate “template reuse” more than “role identity”

Examples of highly repeated patterns across the dataset include:

- pull and slow one enemy
- all attackers gain power this turn
- grant defense plus a shield
- heal all allies
- create a generic spirit token

This makes the set easier to generate, but harder to make competitively legible.

### 3. Some cultures are weakly tagged for gameplay interpretation

A number of cultures use domains that are more philosophical, regional, or lore-specific than gameplay-readable.

Examples:

- `Kashmiri Shaivism`
- `Jain`
- `Tibetan Bon`
- parts of `Ancient Chinese`
- parts of `Korean`

These are flavorful, but the current tag layer does not always translate them into gameplay roles clearly enough.

That means they may need:

- normalized gameplay tags
- stronger support-card direction
- more intentional role assignment

### 4. Some cultures look too “everything at once”

This is dangerous for competitive readability.

A culture should usually make a player think:

- “I know the kind of game this is probably trying to play.”

Not:

- “This could be three unrelated gameplay styles at the same time.”

Too much spread makes counterplay harder to learn and makes matchup prep feel like trivia study.

## What This Means For The Family Model

The family model is still the right direction.

The current dataset does **not** disprove it.

If anything, the dataset shows why the family model is necessary.

It gives us a way to turn:

- mythic abundance

into:

- readable competitive identity

## Practical Conclusion On Character Distribution

### Good enough

- enough legends
- enough flavor range
- enough domain variation
- enough source material for strong signature cards

### Not good enough yet

- too many repeated abilities
- too many mixed identities per culture
- inconsistent gameplay tagging
- not enough distinction between primary plan and secondary lean

## Recommended Target Distribution Per Culture

For a 20-figure culture file, the target should become:

- 1 Signature Character
- 7 to 8 primary-family figures
- 4 to 5 secondary-leaning figures
- 4 to 5 glue figures that support standard gameplay needs
- 2 to 3 flex/flavor figures

This is much better than letting all 20 drift equally across many possible identities.

## Signature Character Readiness

The current dataset is especially well-suited for the “one memorable special character per culture” rule.

Why:

- most cultures already have at least two obvious prestige figures
- many of those figures are famous enough to be remembered easily
- they are the natural place to put the stronger culture-specific gameplay twist

So this part of the plan is strongly supported by the current material.

These look relatively easy to align cleanly:

## Cultures Likely To Need The Most Curation

These look like they need more deliberate gameplay assignment or tag cleanup:

- `Ancient Greek`
- `Ancient Egyptian`
- `Aztec / Mexica`
- `Apache`
- `Arabian Pre-Islamic`
- `Celtic Welsh`
- `Kashmiri Shaivism`
- `Jain`
- `Tibetan Bon`
- `Ancient Chinese`

That does not mean the cultures are bad.

It means they are currently more lore-rich than gameplay-focused.

## The Real Fixes Needed

To make the current character pool match the competitive readability goal, the next fixes should be:

1. Assign every culture a primary family and one secondary leaning.
2. Normalize gameplay tags so lore-heavy domains map into gameplay-readable roles.
3. Reserve bigger rule-bending for one `Signature Character` only.
4. Reduce repeated generic ability patterns inside and across cultures.
5. Rebuild figure roles around clearer gameplay jobs, not just mythology flavor.

## Final Verdict

The current figure distribution is:

- promising
- playable as source material
- not yet tuned for readable high-level competition

So the answer is:

- yes, the material is aligned enough to move forward
- no, the current distribution should not be treated as final

It needs one more meaningful curation layer before it becomes the competitive culture system we want.
