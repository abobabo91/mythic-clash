# Mythic Clash Culture Identity Matrix

Status: locked

This document defines how cultures should feel different without becoming unreadable.

The guiding rule is:

- different plans
- same game grammar

Players should be able to quickly understand what kind of game the opposing culture is trying to play.

They should not need to memorize 100 private subsystems.

## Core Philosophy

Cultures should differ mainly by:

- stat bias
- keyword frequency
- support-card mix
- preferred timing windows
- value vs tempo preference
- token density
- healing density
- recursion density
- relic usage

Cultures should not usually differ by:

- unique resource systems
- unique board rules
- unique timing systems
- private hidden zones
- alternate victory conditions

That means a player can say:

- "this is probably a war-pressure culture"
- "this is probably a wisdom/spell deck"
- "this is probably a death-recursion deck"

and already be mostly oriented.

## The Structure We Should Use

Each culture should have:

- 1 primary gameplay family
- 1 secondary leaning
- 1 signature character with a more memorable, slightly rule-bending ability

This is much better than making every culture fully bespoke.

Why:

- players learn the family first
- then the culture flavor twist
- then the one standout character

That is the right amount of complexity to remember.

## The Eight Gameplay Families

These are not eight "hard factions." They are the readable gameplay buckets that cultures map into.

## 1. Vanguard

Short version:

- combat-first pressure

What players should expect:

- strong bodies
- attack buffs
- strike effects
- proactive combat

Strengths:

- tempo
- board pressure
- combat dominance

Weaknesses:

- can overextend into sweepers
- often weaker at raw card draw
- can struggle if stalled and out-valued

Typical domains:

- war
- kingship
- honor
- strength
- protection

Typical keyword bias:

- Guard
- Overwhelm
- Rush

Typical cultures that can fit:

- Norse / Viking
- Rajput / Rajasthani
- Roman
- Ancient Greek heroic builds
- Inca heroic builds

Signature character style:

- may bend combat rules slightly
- may gain bonus attack cadence
- may convert trades into pressure

## 2. Tempest

Short version:

- spell-tempo and burst pressure

What players should expect:

- direct damage
- fast spells
- tempo swings
- punishing open boards

Strengths:

- reach
- initiative swings
- reactive pressure

Weaknesses:

- can run low on board presence
- often needs good sequencing to avoid burning out
- can be punished by healing and armor

Typical domains:

- storm
- lightning
- fire
- sky
- wrath

Typical keyword bias:

- Rush
- Charge in low quantity
- Freeze in some variants

Typical cultures that can fit:

- Zeus-centered Ancient Greek
- Thor-centered Norse
- Yoruba storm lines
- Shinto storm lines
- Mesoamerican solar-sacrifice tempo shells

Signature character style:

- may bend spell timing or damage distribution
- may reward first spell each turn
- may chain tactical damage

## 3. Sanctuary

Short version:

- heal, protect, stabilize, then win on board

What players should expect:

- healing
- armor
- protection
- efficient answers
- patient board play

Strengths:

- anti-aggro
- strong stabilization
- good attrition tools

Weaknesses:

- can be slower out of the gate
- may have less reach
- can lose if it cannot close

Typical domains:

- sun
- healing
- order
- light
- guardianship

Typical keyword bias:

- Guard
- Ward
- Lifesteal

Typical cultures that can fit:

- Ancient Egyptian solar lines
- Vaishnava Hindu
- Sikh
- Jain
- Zoroastrian

Signature character style:

- may redirect or prevent damage
- may turn healing into board growth
- may create a temporary protection window

## 4. Gravebound

Short version:

- death-value, sacrifice, recursion

What players should expect:

- death triggers
- return from discard
- expendable units
- attrition pressure

Strengths:

- resilience
- grindy value
- excellent recovery after trades

Weaknesses:

- graveyard hate hurts more
- may be slower to seize initiative
- can be vulnerable to silence / banish style effects

Typical domains:

- death
- underworld
- ancestors
- spirits
- judgment

Typical keyword bias:

- Reborn
- Lifesteal
- Guard on some slower units

Typical cultures that can fit:

- Hades-centered Greek underworld builds
- Ancient Egyptian underworld builds
- Haitian Vodou
- Mandaean
- Burmese / Lao death-judgment lines

Signature character style:

- may return once
- may convert death into tempo
- may let sacrifice become reach or board refill

## 5. Tidebound

Short version:

- control-tempo through bounce, repositioning, and pacing

What players should expect:

- return-to-hand effects
- freeze / delay
- movement and slot manipulation
- slower pressure with tactical disruption

Strengths:

- punishes overinvestment
- excellent tempo denial
- good into tall boards

Weaknesses:

- can struggle to hard-kill threats cleanly
- often less explosive
- can lose if it never converts control into victory

Typical domains:

- sea
- water
- moon
- wind
- navigation

Typical keyword bias:

- Stealth in some moon variants
- Freeze effects
- Rush on agile units

Typical cultures that can fit:

- Polynesian / Hawaiian
- Maori sea lines
- Inuit
- Japanese sea/moon lines
- Phoenician

Signature character style:

- may reposition units
- may ignore normal attack lane expectations
- may reset enemy investment

## 6. Oracle

Short version:

- planning, card selection, prediction, value setup

What players should expect:

- draw
- filtering
- discover
- delayed payoff
- efficient answers rather than brute force

Strengths:

- consistency
- flexibility
- strong long-game planning

Weaknesses:

- can be punished before setup pays off
- often weaker on raw stat rate

Typical domains:

- wisdom
- prophecy
- writing
- magic
- stars

Typical keyword bias:

- Ward on key pieces
- Stealth in some mystic builds

Typical cultures that can fit:

- Jewish Mysticism
- Babylonian
- Ancient Chinese
- Kashmiri Shaivism
- Korean prophetic lines

Signature character style:

- may manipulate draw or discover unusually well
- may convert knowledge into a tempo spike
- may bend hand generation in a bounded way

## 7. Wildhost

Short version:

- board swarm, growth, and creature synergy

What players should expect:

- token generation
- wide boards
- buffs
- beasts / spirits / fertility growth

Strengths:

- go-wide pressure
- strong board snowball
- good use of buffs

Weaknesses:

- weak into sweepers
- weak into repeated anti-token tools

Typical domains:

- fertility
- beasts
- forest
- harvest
- life

Typical keyword bias:

- Guard on support bodies
- Overwhelm on finishers

Typical cultures that can fit:

- Celtic nature lines
- Guarani
- Aboriginal Australian life-spirit lines
- some Yoruba fertility lines
- some Mesoamerican growth lines

Signature character style:

- may multiply tokens
- may turn healing or summoning into board growth
- may reward going wide in a memorable way

## 8. Trickster

Short version:

- deception, bounce, stealth, stat twists, tactical disruption

What players should expect:

- hard-to-pin-down units
- temporary theft or redirection effects
- hand and board tricks
- awkward combat math

Strengths:

- punishes rigid play
- great at creating mistakes
- excels in tactical windows

Weaknesses:

- often lower raw stats
- can fail if drawn in the wrong order
- can be weak into simple sturdy boards

Typical domains:

- trickery
- shadows
- illusion
- fox / spider / coyote style cunning
- crossroads

Typical keyword bias:

- Stealth
- Rush
- occasional Freeze

Typical cultures that can fit:

- Akan / Anansi lines
- Norse Loki-style lines
- some Japanese fox-spirit lines
- some Mesoamerican trickster lines
- some Native American trickster lines

Signature character style:

- may alter targeting or combat assumptions slightly
- may copy or redirect effects
- may create high-skill tempo traps

## How Cultures Should Actually Differ

A culture should feel unique because of:

- which family it belongs to
- which secondary leaning it has
- how its support cards express those domains
- which one signature character it has

Example:

- Ancient Greek could be `Vanguard` primary with `Oracle` or `Tempest` secondary.
- Ancient Egyptian could be `Sanctuary` primary with `Gravebound` secondary.
- Norse could be `Vanguard` primary with `Tempest` secondary.
- Jewish Mysticism could be `Oracle` primary with `Sanctuary` secondary.
- Haitian Vodou could be `Gravebound` primary with `Wildhost` secondary.

This is enough variety without forcing players to learn brand-new rules each time.

## The One Special Character Per Culture Idea

I think this is a very good idea.

I would strongly recommend it.

This is the safest place to put the more diverse, culture-defining ability that people will remember.

Why it works:

- players only need to remember one major outlier per culture
- it preserves the feeling that the culture has a face
- it gives flavor room without making the whole card pool weird

But it needs constraints.

## Rule For Signature Characters

Each culture gets exactly one `Signature Character`.

Rules:

- always unique
- always 1 copy per deck
- always visually marked clearly
- strong identity card
- not the highest raw stat card by default
- power budget is mostly in the ability, not raw rate

This should be the card players learn to recognize.

## What Kind Of Rule-Breaking Is Healthy

Healthy rule-bending:

- slightly unusual timing access
- one extra attack under conditions
- unusual targeting permission
- stronger recursion than normal
- converting one resource into another
- repositioning or board-slot manipulation
- bounded copying / discovering
- temporary protection bypass

Unhealthy rule-bending:

- alternate win conditions
- private resources
- immunity to major parts of the rules
- endless free card loops
- repeated extra turns
- no-counterplay burst kill text

So yes:

- diverse

But not:

- unbounded

## Signature Character Design Rules

Each Signature Character should follow all of these:

### 1. One major twist only

The card can bend one axis strongly.

Examples of one axis:

- attack cadence
- targeting
- recursion
- timing
- summoning
- spell amplification

Not two or three axes at once.

### 2. Still answerable

The opponent should still be able to beat it with:

- removal
- silence
- pressure
- board positioning
- sideboard tech

If the only answer is "already know the exact card and pray," the design is bad.

### 3. Strong identity, not strongest finisher

This card should be:

- memorable
- build-around
- important

But not automatically:

- the biggest body
- the strongest endgame bomb
- the only card that matters

### 4. It should reinforce the culture family

The signature card should not pull the culture into a totally different game.

It should say:

- "this is the most intense version of this culture's plan"

not:

- "this culture suddenly plays a completely different genre of deck"

### 5. It should create a recognizable question

Good signature characters create questions like:

- can I kill this before it snowballs?
- can I play around its response window?
- can I deny the death trigger?
- can I stop the relic synergy?

That is good competitive texture.

## Signature Character Patterns By Family

## Vanguard signature pattern

Theme:

- decisive combat leader

Allowed twist:

- may attack twice, but only if it killed a Figure or attacked a Figure first

Why it works:

- memorable
- still board-based
- still answerable

## Tempest signature pattern

Theme:

- storm-caller or spell-war leader

Allowed twist:

- the first Fast spell you play each turn gets a bonus rider

Why it works:

- feels magical
- rewards planning
- not a private resource system

## Sanctuary signature pattern

Theme:

- protector, solar ruler, healer-judge

Allowed twist:

- when you restore Health, gain a secondary effect once each turn

Why it works:

- reinforces sustain identity
- stays readable

## Gravebound signature pattern

Theme:

- lord of death, ferryman, ancestor-queen

Allowed twist:

- the first friendly Figure that dies each turn leaves behind a stronger spirit or returns to hand later

Why it works:

- iconic
- counterable with silence / banish / pressure

## Tidebound signature pattern

Theme:

- sea-lord, moon navigator, wave sovereign

Allowed twist:

- may bounce or reposition when attacking or on summon

Why it works:

- very flavorful
- tactical, not system-breaking

## Oracle signature pattern

Theme:

- seer, sage, keeper of law

Allowed twist:

- once each turn, discover from a tight legal pool after meeting a condition

Why it works:

- high skill
- bounded randomness

## Wildhost signature pattern

Theme:

- earth mother, beast king, fertility spirit

Allowed twist:

- token or summon effects scale once per turn

Why it works:

- reinforces the board plan
- exciting but still visible

## Trickster signature pattern

Theme:

- illusionist, fox, spider, liar-god

Allowed twist:

- may redirect a targeted effect or swap stats this combat once per turn

Why it works:

- memorable
- tactical
- still within known game grammar

## Good Example Shape

Good signature card feeling:

- "Athena lets one protected ally ignore the next attack and become stronger"
- "Thor turns the first Fast storm spell each turn into a wider strike"
- "Hades makes the first dead ally each turn return as a shade"
- "Amaterasu rewards healing and protection with board light"
- "Anansi can twist one combat or targeting interaction each turn"

These feel different, but the player still understands what kind of game is happening.

## Bad Example Shape

Bad signature card feeling:

- "this card uses a private fate counter resource"
- "this card attacks from a hidden zone"
- "this card changes the entire win condition"
- "this card cannot be targeted, damaged, or raced"
- "this card starts a separate mini-combat system"

Those are memorable, but they are bad for competitive readability.

## Practical Recommendation For The Project

I recommend:

1. Build the 8 gameplay families first.
2. Assign each culture a primary family and one secondary leaning.
3. Keep most cards inside the shared card grammar.
4. Give each culture exactly one `Signature Character`.
5. Let that one card be the main culture-specific rule-bender.

This will give Mythic Clash:

- readable matchups
- distinct culture identity
- good player memory load
- room for special flavor

without turning the game into a study burden.

## What This Means For Card Design

Most cards in a culture should answer:

- what is this culture trying to do normally?

The Signature Character should answer:

- what is the most iconic, slightly unfair-looking but still healthy version of that plan?

That is the right place for the more dramatic design.
