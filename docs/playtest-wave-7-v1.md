# Playtest Wave 7 v1

Last updated: 2026-05-04

Wave 7 translated:

- `hopi`
- `toltec`

Both packs:

- export cleanly
- validate as legal `24`-card lists
- passed rollout smoke tests after one sample-deck correction on `Hopi`

## Hopi

Identity read:

- `Sanctuary` primary
- `Oracle` secondary
- ritual rain control with guards, healing, and steady wisdom draw

Smoke record:

- `1-1` vs `Ancient Greek`
- `2-0` vs `Haitian Vodou`
- `2-0` vs `Inuit`

Both-seat spot check vs `Ancient Greek`:

- `1-3`

Main gameplay feel:

- calm and easy to parse
- much healthier once the sample deck leaned on real midgame anchors instead of too much small healing glue
- still a little seat-sensitive into the strongest tempo pressure
- good visible stabilization without private subsystems

The first Hopi sample deck was too soft because it skipped its best `5`-cost anchor and overplayed redundant heal bodies.

After the deck correction, the culture became rollout-healthy without changing the pack identity.

## Toltec

Identity read:

- `Oracle` primary
- `Tempest` secondary
- wisdom-tempo pressure with direct removal, rush follow-through, and selective recursion

Smoke record:

- `2-0` vs `Ancient Greek`
- `1-1` vs `Haitian Vodou`
- `2-0` vs `Inuit`

Both-seat spot check vs `Ancient Greek`:

- `2-2`

Main gameplay feel:

- proactive and readable
- strong midgame pressure
- healthy use of card selection and removal
- strong from the play, but not obviously broken once seat bias is checked

Toltec looked suspiciously strong on the first smoke pass, but the both-seat check showed that part of that result was first-seat pressure rather than a clearly broken pack.

## Practical meaning

Wave 7 is a useful stress-case success.

`Hopi` proves we can rescue a rough-compatibility culture through pack curation and deck construction rather than inventing strange mechanics.

`Toltec` proves a rough-compatibility culture can still land in a strong, readable tempo shell without becoming hard to understand.
